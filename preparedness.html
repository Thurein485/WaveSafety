HOME SAFETY

Protect your home from flooding

What you should do

  • Check whether your home is in a flood-prone area.
  • Keep drains and gutters clear of rubbish when it is safe to do so.
  • Move important documents, electronics, and valuables to higher shelves.
  • Keep emergency phone numbers and important documents in a waterproof bag.
  • Know the safest route to higher ground or an official evacuation shelter.
Never enter floodwater to protect belongings. Your safety comes first.

EMERGENCY SUPPLIES

Pack a flood emergency kit

Essential supplies

  • Drinking water and ready-to-eat food.
  • First-aid kit and necessary medicines.
  • Flashlight, spare batteries, and a power bank.
  • Phone, emergency contact list, and a battery-powered radio if available.
  • Copies of important documents stored in a waterproof bag.
  • Extra clothes, hygiene items, and basic supplies for children or older family members.
Keep your kit somewhere easy to reach. Do not delay evacuation to collect supplies.

FAMILY SAFETY

Create a plan everyone understands

Before flooding begins

  • Choose a safe meeting place on higher ground.
  • Identify evacuation routes and nearby official shelters.
  • Write down important phone numbers in case your phone battery dies.
  • Choose an out-of-area contact whom family members can call or message.
  • Plan how to help children, older adults, people with disabilities, and pets.
  • Follow official warnings and leave early when authorities advise evacuation.
Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwater. It may be deeper or faster-moving than it looks.

REMEMBER

Important flood safety tips

Keep these safety rules in mind before and during a flood.

Stay out of floodwater

Floodwater may hide holes, debris, strong currents, or electrical hazards. Do not walk or drive through it.

Follow official warnings

Listen to local authorities and official weather updates. Evacuate promptly when instructed.

Avoid electrical hazards

Stay away from fallen power lines and electrical equipment exposed to water. Do not touch wet electrical devices.

STAY INFORMED

Check official weather updates

Monitor warnings from the Department of Meteorology and Hydrology and follow local emergency instructions.
